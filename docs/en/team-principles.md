# Team management principles

[简体中文](../团队管理原则.md) | **English**

Status: Proposal. Intended for small and medium engineering teams working primarily through GitHub. Test the practices against the team's actual work.

## Set priorities through shared goals

Define the user or business problem for each cycle before choosing tasks. Make explicit tradeoffs when demand exceeds capacity. An urgent addition should identify which commitments move. Product and engineering should assess scope, dependencies, and risk together; sustained overtime is not a substitute for capacity decisions.

## Clarify responsibility and delegate decisions

Each task has one outcome owner who follows development, review, validation, and delivery through to completion. Technical, product, testing, and release roles remain responsible for their professional judgments. Give module owners appropriate decision authority and define when material risks need escalation, rather than routing every routine decision through one person.

## Support status with evidence

Use Issues, PRs, validation, and release records to explain progress. “In progress” should include remaining questions and the next action. A blocker needs a cause, an owner for resolution, and a review time. Written records should reduce repeated conversations without becoming an extra daily reporting burden.

## Keep parallel work manageable

Finish work already started. As an initial experiment, give each developer one main development task; address review and testing queues together. This is a starting point, not a universal quota. Maintenance, pairing, and the nature of a task may require adjustments.

## Share quality responsibility throughout delivery

Developers provide validation evidence, reviewers examine key logic, acceptance owners check goals, and service owners observe production behavior. Each stage contributes to quality. High-risk changes need more validation; low-risk changes need proportionate checks.

## Turn retrospectives into system improvements

Discuss facts, conditions, and impact rather than attributing everything to individual carelessness. Choose a small number of concrete improvements, each with an owner and follow-up date. Heavy processes, unreliable tests, unclear information, and permission design can all be system problems worth changing.

## Support growth and reduce single-person dependencies

Assign backup owners for critical modules. Spread knowledge through pairing, review rotation, and operating guides. Use regular one-to-one conversations to agree on skills to develop and support needed. Assess contribution through concrete examples of problem solving, quality, collaboration, knowledge sharing, and growth.

## Adoption

Start with one team and one delivery cycle, focusing on the most important problem. Record waiting, rework, and collaboration feedback. At the end, decide which practices to keep, adjust, or stop. Assign an owner to maintain the rules and remove rules that no longer help.
